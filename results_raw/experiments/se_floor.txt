============================================================================================
HOW MUCH CAN MORE SPLITS BUY?  The Nadeau-Bengio floor.
  NB SE = s * sqrt(1/K + f/(1-f));  only the 1/K term shrinks with K.
============================================================================================
  NB SE as a multiple of s, and the smallest resolvable effect (1.96 SE):
       K                f=0.20                f=0.30                f=0.40
               SE/s      MDE/s       SE/s      MDE/s       SE/s      MDE/s
       3     0.7638     1.4969     0.8729     1.7108     1.0000     1.9600
       5     0.6708     1.3148     0.7928     1.5539     0.9309     1.8246
      10     0.5916     1.1595     0.7270     1.4250     0.8756     1.7161
      20     0.5477     1.0735     0.6918     1.3559     0.8466     1.6592
      50     0.5196     1.0184     0.6698     1.3127     0.8287     1.6241
     120     0.5083     0.9962     0.6610     1.2955     0.8216     1.6103
    1000     0.5010     0.9819     0.6554     1.2846     0.8171     1.6015
  1000000     0.5000     0.9800     0.6547     1.2831     0.8165     1.6003

  The last row is the limit.  At the project's f = 0.3 the NB SE never
  falls below 0.6547 s, so an effect below 1.283 s is unresolvable at the
  5% level no matter how many splits are run.

  NAIVE SE, for contrast, is s/sqrt(K) and has no floor:
       K    naive SE/s   NB SE/s (f=0.3)    ratio
       3        0.5774            0.8729     1.51
       5        0.4472            0.7928     1.77
      10        0.3162            0.7270     2.30
      20        0.2236            0.6918     3.09
      50        0.1414            0.6698     4.74
     120        0.0913            0.6610     7.24

  At K = 20 the honest interval is 3.09x the naive one, and at K = 120 it
  is 7.24x.  Reporting sd/sqrt(K) on overlapping splits therefore gets
  MORE wrong as the split count rises, which is the opposite of the
  intuition that more splits make a naive error bar safer.

============================================================================================
WHAT THIS MEANS FOR E6d's SIGN FLIP
============================================================================================
  The two negative cells at 120 replications were
    T=80   D_T = -0.00972   NB SE 0.04906
    T=160  D_T = -0.02124   NB SE 0.04145
  Their across-split standard deviations are therefore
    s = 0.07422  ->  floor on NB SE = 0.04859,  smallest resolvable |D_T| = 0.09523  (observed 0.00972)
    s = 0.06271  ->  floor on NB SE = 0.04106,  smallest resolvable |D_T| = 0.08046  (observed 0.02124)

  Both observed effects are below their own floors, so NO number of
  splits resolves them.  The evidence for the crossover is therefore the
  PATTERN -- a sign change that moves right as n grows, plus E9's
  independent nuisance-dimension result -- and the paper must present it
  that way rather than promising significance that this design cannot
  deliver.

  The honest fix for a specific cell is more DATA or an independent test
  set, not more resamples of the same cohort.
